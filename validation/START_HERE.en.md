# Sightline · First-round validation pack

This is not a request for praise. It is a check on whether Sightline actually changes a design decision.

## One sentence for testers

Treat this as a pre-publish visual check: identify what must stay legible, then see whether it still works on a small screen, in grayscale, and in approximate color-vision simulations.

## Suggested testers

- Graphic, brand, UI, or content designers;
- Marketers, product managers, or reviewers responsible for social graphics, campaign pages, or product acceptance;
- People with low vision or color-vision differences who are comfortable giving public feedback.

Do not use client, unreleased, or personally identifying images.

## Five-minute script

### Test 01: low-contrast social graphic

Import `examples/01-low-contrast-social.svg`.

1. Mark the large headline and booking button.
2. Switch to small-screen, grayscale, red-weak, and green-weak views.
3. Sample the headline/background and button-label/button-background pairs.
4. Say what you would change, and why.

Observe whether the tester can find marking and A/B sampling unaided, and whether the tool helps them notice a real legibility issue.

### Test 02: status conveyed only by color

Import `examples/02-color-only-status.svg`.

1. Mark the two statuses.
2. Switch to red-weak and green-weak views.
3. Decide whether success and failure remain immediately distinguishable.
4. Suggest an improvement that does not rely on color alone.

Observe whether the tester understands that a possible color conflict is a risk hint, not an automatic failure.

### Test 03: a clear version

Import `examples/03-clear-information.svg`.

1. Mark the title, time, and primary CTA.
2. Sample one body-text/background pair.
3. Check whether the tool stays proportionate rather than alarming.

Observe whether the tester understands that Sightline is not a “higher score is better” tool.

## Ask only these five questions at the end

1. At which step did you hesitate or get stuck?
2. Which view first made you notice a problem?
3. Which design decision would you actually change?
4. Which hint was too vague, alarming, or unhelpful?
5. Would you open Sightline again before your next delivery? Why?

## Record only anonymous information

- Tester role;
- Whether the three tasks were completed smoothly;
- Whether they proposed a real design change;
- Most and least useful feature;
- A short paraphrase of their feedback.

Do not collect images, names, client names, accounts, contact details, or other personal information.

## First-round success criteria

With 6–10 testers, at least half should complete marking + view switching + A/B sampling without explanation, and at least four should identify a design decision they would actually change. Only then is it worth preparing for a wider public release.
